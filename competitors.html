The One Row That Decides Commercial Work
Accreditation is a procurement gate, not a marketing badge

On domestic work a homeowner might never ask. On commercial and industrial work, main contractors, councils, housing associations and facilities managers routinely require CHAS, SafeContractor or Constructionline before a firm can even be added to a tender list.

Requirements vary by buyer and contract — this is not a rule we can state for your market. But three of your four nearest rivals have decided it is worth displaying, and you are the one asking how to win more of exactly that work.

If you hold them, this is a twenty-minute fix — logos and registration numbers on every page. If you do not, that is the single highest-value conversation we could have, and it matters more than anything else in either document.

Where You Can Win
This is not a strong field, and that is the opportunity
  • Only one of the four is measuring anything. Glasby has GA4; nobody else has any analytics at all. A firm that can see which enquiries come from where has an advantage none of them can currently match.
  • Three of four publish no sitemap and almost no pages. Glasby's 23 pages is the ceiling in this market. That is a low bar to clear.
  • Nobody is retargeting. No pixel found on any of the four.
  • You have a landline, a mobile, two named email addresses and a real industrial-estate address. That reads as an established firm — more than some of these publish.
  • You do commercial and domestic. The domestic work keeps the lights on while the commercial pipeline builds, which is a luxury a commercial-only contractor does not have.
And where they are ahead

Glasby states 45 years and publishes 23 pages with proper addresses, analytics and a tappable number. They are the only firm here behaving like they intend to grow, and everything they have done is cheap and copyable — which cuts both ways.

What We Could Not Check
Marked honestly rather than guessed
  • Whether any firm here, including you, actually holds the accreditations displayed — we read websites, not registers.
  • Everyone's Google Business Profile, reviews and map position, including yours. On local trade work that is where a lot of enquiries begin and we have no reliable way to measure it.
  • Anyone's revenue, staffing, tender list membership, pricing or win rate, including yours.
  • Whether any of them advertise on Google, which is a separate auction we have not assessed.
  • Every commercial electrical contractor in South Yorkshire. This is the set we could find and verify, not a complete census, and it excludes the national M&E contractors who bid the largest work.

Only one competitor here is really trying. That is a rare position to be handed.

But the accreditation row is the one that decides whether you get asked to quote at all.
