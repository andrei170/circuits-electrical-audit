Your Website Still Says "Tagline" Where The Company Description Should Be.

856
words across your entire website, all six pages

You want more commercial and industrial work. That is exactly the buyer who checks a website before they invite you to quote.

The tab at the top of your homepage currently reads "CIrcuits Electrical – Tagline". "Tagline" is the placeholder WordPress ships with. Your commercial page is 168 words long and lives at an address that reads ?page_id=401.

All six pages read 22 September 2026
Finding 1 — The Title Tag, Which Is The First Thing Anyone Sees
What the browser tab and Google both show: CIrcuits Electrical – Tagline Two separate problems in five words: "Tagline" the WordPress default placeholder, never replaced "CIrcuits" a capital I in the middle of your own company name What it should say: Commercial & Industrial Electrical Contractors | Rotherham & South Yorkshire
This is the single line Google shows in its results

Before anybody reads a word of your site, they see that tab. "Tagline" tells a facilities manager that nobody has looked at this website in a very long time — and the typo in your own name is the kind of detail a careful buyer notices and quietly holds against you.

It is a ten-minute fix. It has been there for years.

Finding 2 — Your Web Addresses Contain No Words At All
Every page on your site: Home circuitselectrical.co.uk/ About Us circuitselectrical.co.uk/?page_id=398 Domestic circuitselectrical.co.uk/?page_id=400 Commercial circuitselectrical.co.uk/?page_id=401 Projects circuitselectrical.co.uk/?page_id=558 Contact circuitselectrical.co.uk/?page_id=79 What they should be: /commercial-electrical-contractors/ /industrial-electrical-installations/ /rotherham/ /doncaster/ /sheffield/
WordPress calls this setting "Plain". It is the one you are on.

Google reads the words in a web address as a signal of what the page is about. Yours contain a number and nothing else. Your commercial page could be about anything — the address gives Google nothing to work with.

It is a single setting in WordPress, though it needs redirects putting in at the same time so nothing breaks. This is the second cheapest fix on the list and one of the largest.

Finding 3 — Six Pages, 856 Words, And 168 Of Them Are Your Commercial Offer
Home 130 words About Us 212 words Commercial 168 words <<< the work you want more of Projects 161 words Domestic 133 words Contact 52 words Whole site 856 words Pages naming a town you serve: 0 Rotherham, Doncaster, Sheffield, Barnsley, Mexborough
A main contractor cannot tell what you are capable of

168 words is shorter than this section. There is nothing about the size of job you take, the sectors you work in, your team, your certifications, or a single named project — even though you have a page called Projects.

You described yourselves as providing "a nationwide service to both commercial and domestic customers". A buyer reading 168 words cannot distinguish you from a one-man band, and you have four or five people.

Finding 4 — The Copyright Says 2017
At the foot of every page: Circuits Electrical Ltd © 2017 | All Rights Reserved Elsewhere on the site: "providing a nationwide services" (grammar, on the homepage) No enquiry form except on Contact (52 words long) No tappable phone link anywhere 01709 590994 is plain text
Nine years is a long time for a date to sit wrong

It is a tiny detail and it does real damage. A procurement officer checking three contractors sees 2017 and wonders whether you are still trading. Combined with "Tagline" in the title, the impression is a business that has stopped paying attention.

We do not believe that is true of you — you have four or five people and you are looking to grow. That is precisely why the gap matters.

Finding 5 — Nothing Is Measured, And No Accreditation Is Shown
Tracking across all six pages: Google Analytics not found Google Tag Manager not found Google Ads tag not found Meta pixel not found Structured data none of any kind Meta description none Accreditations displayed on your site: NICEIC / NAPIT / ELECSA none found CHAS none found SafeContractor none found Constructionline none found
This is the finding that matters most for commercial work

Three of the four South Yorkshire competitors we checked display accreditations. S & G Electrical shows NICEIC and CHAS. HEC shows NICEIC and CHAS. A Coole shows CHAS and SafeContractor.

CHAS, SafeContractor and Constructionline are not decoration on commercial work — they are frequently the gate. Plenty of main contractors and public bodies will not add a firm to a tender list without one.

We are not saying you do not hold them. We cannot tell, because your website does not say. And that is the point: if you hold them, they are invisible to every buyer who checks. If you do not, that is the bigger conversation — and it is the first thing we would want to know.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Fix the title, the copyright and the typo. Half an hour of work that changes the first impression of every visitor and every search result.
  • Turn the addresses into words and redirect the old ones, so a commercial page actually says "commercial" in its address.
  • Put your credentials on the site — whichever of NICEIC, CHAS, SafeContractor or Constructionline you hold, with the registration numbers. This is the one that unlocks tender work.
  • Write the commercial and industrial pages properly — sectors, job sizes, named projects, team size, insurance levels. 168 words becomes a page a buyer can act on.
  • Then build the towns and put the meter on, so you can see which work is coming from where.

You have four or five electricians and a website that reads like a placeholder.

Commercial buyers check. That is the entire gap between the work you get and the work you want.
